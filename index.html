What they’d been afraid to say

Daniel set the remote aside. He told her that sometimes, just as they started kissing, he was already wondering whether he’d be able to keep an erection. He admitted that he’d started making excuses because he didn’t know how to explain what was happening.

A closer look at the ingredients

The product brief describes a blend of collagen, a vitamin, an amino acid, botanical extracts, and a mineral. Use the current label to confirm which ingredients are in the version being offered and how much is in each serving.

Hydrolyzed equine collagen

The brief highlights collagen described as being of Percheron origin. Look for the ingredient name, its stated source, and the amount per serving on the current label.

L-arginine and liposomal vitamin C

Epimedium, tongkat ali, and tribulus

The brief mentions three botanicals, including epimedium, also called horny goat weed. Look for their botanical names and check whether each has a listed amount or appears in a blend with a combined total.

How to compare

How to read the supplement label

Start with the Supplement Facts panel. Look for the serving size, ingredient forms, and amounts per serving.

Start with the serving size

Put the two panels side by side and check how many capsules make up a serving in each product. Then compare the amount of each ingredient per serving. A larger bottle may simply contain a product that calls for more capsules per serving.

Check how blends are listed

A single amount for the whole blend tells you its combined weight. When the panel lists amounts separately, it’s easier to compare individual ingredients with another formula or with a product you already use.

See what was studied

If a seller cites a study to support a benefit, look for the product name and formula used in that study. That helps you tell whether the research covers the supplement you’re considering or an ingredient studied in a different context.
